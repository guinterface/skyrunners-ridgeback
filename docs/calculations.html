How we chose the size of the drone, then each check of the drawn design with the equation, our numbers and where it lives in the sheets. Sea level, standard day, unless noted.

Carbon redesign and the team's requirements (estimate)

On 20 Nov we decided to build the structure in carbon fibre instead of printing it, keeping printed parts only for connectors, feet and the electronics plate. These numbers come from analysis/cf_redesign.py (results in cf_redesign.md), using the same equations and inputs as the sheets. Every carbon mass is an estimate until parts are weighed. The 3D view, the mass sheet and the mass and performance numbers at the top of this site still show the printed design until the CAD is redrawn in carbon (the costs already include the carbon materials).

  • No wing and battery size we swept meets every requirement with a 1.5 kg payload. Keeping 1.5 kg, 60 min and 65 km/h gives about 9.2 kg (wing 0.65 m², span 2.55 m, 6S6P).
  • At 8 kg, 60 min and 65 km/h the payload can be at most about 776 g (wing 0.60 m², 6S5P), and thrust/weight is then 1.98, still short of 2.0.
  • Thrust/weight 2.0 needs about 4.9 kg of thrust from each lift motor; the MN5008 with 18 inch props gives 4.2 kg. Bigger lift motors or props are needed, and their extra mass is not counted yet.
  • How much the wing twists with the tube spar alone depends on how the tube is wound, which the store does not say: 0.4° at 65 km/h and 1.1° at 30 m/s if it has ±45° plies, but 1.6° and 4.3° if it is a 0/90 weave or pultruded. Above about 2° the wing needs a molded carbon leading edge (D-box). Still open: ask HaoZhong for the layup, or twist-test one wing panel by hand.
  • Not in the cost total: bigger lift motors for thrust/weight 2, the spot welder and battery tools. About $455 of the total is our estimate, not a quote (carbon cut parts $250 until SendCutSend quotes our DXF files, shop supplies $125, foam $50, the 0.5 mm trailing-edge sheet $30).

Open with the team: which requirement gives (weight, payload or endurance), what the 1.5 kg payload is, and whether they want tilt rotors (their table mentions it).

How we chose the size

The order is the usual one: payload first, then the mission sets the energy, then motors and battery, then the total mass sets the wing. Each step links to the file that does it.

  1. Payload. What the drone exists to carry: Payload computer 50 g; Payload regulator 10 g; Thermal camera 25 g; Colour camera 60 g; 1610 nm spot sensor 80 g; Air temperature and humidity 5 g. Now 230 g in total; the first sizing used 450 g to leave margin. Sheet 02, tab Mass_CG (payload rows), data/parts.toml.
  2. Mission, which fixes the energy. Three flights on one charge (dawn, mid-morning, midday), 15 min over a 2 ha site, up to 1 km out, and at least 80 min of loiter with 20 % left in reserve. Distance never limits us: one charge flies about 69 km against a 1 km radius, so endurance and hovering size the battery, not range. Design numbers, Sheet 04, tab Mission_Energy.
  3. Lift motors and propellers from the maker's own test table (T-Motor MN5008 KV340 with P18x6.1): power needed for each gram of thrust, and the most thrust it gives. The rule: four motors must lift at least 1.5 times the weight on a hot day at 800 m. Step 6, step 7, Sheet 04, tab Lift_Hover.
  4. Sweep wing area and battery together. For every wing from 0.40 to 0.70 m² and every pack from 6S3P to 6S6P: mass = structure (scaled by wing area from our earlier 3.35 m design) + battery (6 × P cells of 70 g, +8 %) + the fixed parts (payload, avionics, motors, ESCs, servos, wiring). Then stall speed, loiter time and hover thrust for each of the 24 options. analysis/mini_sizing.py (the code), mini_sizing.md (the full table).
  5. Pick the cheapest that passes (stall ≤ 15 m/s, hot-day thrust ≥ 1.5 × weight, ≥ 80 min even with 40 % more drag than expected): wing 0.50 m², span 2.24 m, 6S6P, predicted 8.90 kg and 85–100 min. One size up (0.55 m², 6S6P) is too heavy to hover on a hot day (1.48); one pack smaller (6S5P) gives only 75 min with the extra drag.
  6. Draw it, then check it with the drawn numbers (steps 1 to 9 below). The CAD weighs 8.92 kg with the payload, but its drag, summed part by part, is CD0 = 0.063, well above the 0.025–0.035 the sweep assumed; the motors, landing feet and stopped propellers make about 45 % of it. So today: loiter 57 min (target 80), 2.7 flights per charge (target 3), stall 15.5 m/s (target 15), hot-day thrust 1.54 × weight (needs 1.5). Sheet 03, tab Drag_Buildup, sheets/build_sheet.py (builds every sheet).
  7. Then the requirements changed and the loop was run again. The team asked for a 1.5 kg payload, 60 min at 65 km/h, 5 km, 8 kg all-up and hover thrust at least 2 × weight, and the structure moved to carbon. Same order: payload, then energy for 60 min at 65 km/h, then battery and lift motors, then the wing from the total mass. Result: with 1.5 kg on board it weighs 9.1 kg (more than 8 kg), and the lightest wing that meets endurance, range and speed is 0.65 m² (2.55 m span, 9.2 kg). Kept at 8 kg, the payload can be at most 776 g. Today's motors give 1.74 × weight, short of 2, so bigger lift motors are being compared. Carbon redesign, analysis/cf_redesign.py, analysis/lift_motor_tw.py.

1. Weight

Everything the rotors and the wing must carry: the CAD parts plus the sensor payload.

\[W = m\,g\]
\[W = 8.92\ \text{kg} \times 9.81\ \text{m/s}^2\]

W = 87.4 N

In the sheets: Sheet 02, tab Mass_CG

2. Stall speed

The slowest the wing can fly. The wing's maximum lift coefficient is 0.9 times the SD7037 airfoil's (Raymer).

\[V_s = \sqrt{\dfrac{2W}{\rho\, S\, C_{L,\max}}}\]
\[V_s = \sqrt{\dfrac{2 \times 87.4\ \text{N}}{1.225\ \text{kg/m}^3 \times 0.50\ \text{m}^2 \times 1.186}}\]

Vs = 15.5 m/s, against the 15 m/s we wanted.

In the sheets: Sheet 03, tab Aero_Cruise

3. Drag of the whole drone

Summed over every component: skin friction on the wing, tail, fuselage, booms and nacelles, and pressure drag on the motors, feet and stopped propellers.

\[C_{D0} = \dfrac{\sum C_f\, FF\, Q\, S_{wet} + \sum C_d\, A_{front}}{S}\]
\[C_{D0} = 0.0626\]

The motors, landing feet and stopped propellers make about 45 % of it.

In the sheets: Sheet 03, tab Drag_Buildup

4. Loiter speed and drag

The drone loiters at 1.3 times the stall speed, which here is faster than its minimum-power speed.

\[\begin{aligned} V &= 1.3\,V_s \\ C_L &= \dfrac{2W}{\rho V^2 S} \\ C_D &= C_{D0} + K C_L^2 \\ D &= \tfrac12 \rho V^2 S\, C_D \end{aligned}\]
\[V = 20.2,\quad C_L = 0.702,\quad C_D = 0.0626 + 0.0420\times0.702^2 = 0.0833\]

Drag D = 10.4 N at 20.2 m/s.

In the sheets: Sheet 03, tab Aero_Cruise

5. Loiter power

Thrust power through the pusher (efficiency η = 0.55, assumed), plus 25 W for the flight electronics and the mission sensors.

\[P = \dfrac{D\,V}{\eta} + P_{av}\]
\[P = \dfrac{10.4 \times 20.2}{0.55} + 25\]

P = 406 W

In the sheets: Sheet 03, tab Aero_Cruise

6. Hover power

From T-Motor's own MN5008 test table, fitted as a power law and corrected for air density.

\[P_{motor} = e^{c}\, T^{k} \sqrt{\rho_{test}/\rho},\qquad P_{hover} = 4P_{motor} + P_{av}\]
\[T = 2,229\ \text{g per motor},\quad k = 1.490\]

Phover = 1,151 W at sea level, 1,236 W on a hot day at 800 m.

In the sheets: Sheet 04, tab Lift_Hover

7. Thrust to weight

Can the four rotors hold the drone steady in gusts on a hot day at 800 m? We require at least 1.5.

\[\dfrac{T}{W} = \dfrac{4\,T_{max}\,(V_{load}/V_{test})^2\,(\rho/\rho_{test})}{W}\]
\[\dfrac{T}{W} = \dfrac{4 \times 33.6\ \text{N}}{87.4\ \text{N}}\]

T/W = 1.54 on the hot day (1.78 at sea level).

In the sheets: Sheet 04, tab Lift_Hover

8. Energy and flights per charge

Usable energy keeps the cells between 95 % and 10 % charge and leaves 20 % in reserve.

\[E_u = E_{pack}\times 0.85 \times 0.8,\qquad n = \dfrac{E_u}{E_{hover} + E_{climb} + P\,(t_{survey} + t_{transit})}\]
\[E_u = 648 \times 0.85 \times 0.8 = 441\ \text{Wh},\quad n = \dfrac{441}{165}\]

2.7 flights per charge; loiter time 57 min.

In the sheets: Sheet 04, tab Mission_Energy

9. Range

Distance on one charge at loiter speed. Shown to make the point that it never limits us.

\[\text{range} = V\, t_{loiter}\]
\[\text{range} = 20.2\ \text{m/s} \times 3,443\ \text{s}\]

69 km, against a 1 km operating radius.

In the sheets: Sheet 04, tab Mission_Energy
